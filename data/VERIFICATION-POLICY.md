# StudyBridge data quality and verification policy

StudyBridge exists to reduce the time students spend searching across scattered sources. Trust is more important than directory size.

## Non-negotiable publishing rules

1. Never invent or infer a tuition amount, degree offering, admission requirement, deadline, scholarship, language of instruction, visa rule, or work entitlement.
2. Missing information must be displayed as "Not yet verified" (or the equivalent in the selected language), never as zero, free, unavailable, or a guessed estimate.
3. Separate institution-level facts from programme-level facts. Tuition and entry requirements are usually programme-, degree-, student-category-, and intake-specific.
4. Keep the original source URL, date checked, academic year/intake, currency, and fee basis with every time-sensitive fact.
5. Distinguish international-student fees from domestic fees.
6. If official sources conflict, do not silently choose one. Flag the record for review and record both sources.
7. Show users when information was last checked and provide a direct official link so they can confirm before applying.
8. Do not claim that StudyBridge guarantees admission, visa approval, scholarship eligibility, or employment.

## Source hierarchy

Use sources in this order:
1. Official university programme page, admissions page, fee schedule, or scholarship page.
2. Official university international-student office or registrar page.
3. Official national government immigration, education, or credential-recognition authority.
4. Official national accreditation or quality-assurance agency.
5. Recognized intergovernmental or sector databases for institution discovery and cross-checking.
6. Reputable secondary sources only as discovery leads; never as the sole evidence for a published fee, admission rule, visa rule, or scholarship.

## Scholarship verification rules
- List a scholarship only when an official provider, government, university, or recognized funding body publishes the opportunity.
- Record eligible nationalities/residency, study levels, fields, host-country restrictions, award value, what costs are covered, duration, application route, deadline or cycle, and official source URL.
- Do not describe a scholarship as fully funded unless the official source explicitly confirms the covered costs.
- Do not assume a student is eligible based only on their destination country or nationality; show the published eligibility criteria and ask students to confirm with the provider.
- Mark closed or expired opportunities clearly; do not silently show them as currently open.
- Recheck each opportunity for every application cycle and record the date checked.

## Evidence record for each programme

- Institution ID and official programme title
- Qualification/degree level
- Programme URL
- Campus/location (if relevant)
- Teaching language and evidence URL
- International tuition amount, currency, fee basis, academic year/intake, and evidence URL
- Entry requirements and evidence URL
- Application deadline/intake and evidence URL
- Scholarship link, eligibility and deadline (if applicable)
- Date checked, reviewer, verification status, and review notes

## Status meanings

- `not_verified`: no official evidence has been checked yet.
- `official_source_verified`: the exact claim was checked against a relevant official source on the recorded date.
- `expired`: the source or academic year is no longer current and must be rechecked.
- `conflict_review`: official sources disagree or the evidence is ambiguous.

## Update cadence

- Tuition, deadlines, scholarships, admissions requirements, visa and work rules: recheck for each intake or academic year, and whenever an official source announces a change.
- Institution name, official domain and accreditation/recognition status: recheck at least annually and whenever a national authority reports a change.
- Never carry forward an old fee or visa rule into a new academic year without checking it again.

## Student-facing transparency

Every result should make clear:
- what is confirmed,
- what is missing or still being checked,
- when it was last checked,
- who publishes the original information (the institution or government),
- and where the student can verify it directly.

The official institution or government source is authoritative. StudyBridge is a discovery and organization tool, not a substitute for official application instructions.
